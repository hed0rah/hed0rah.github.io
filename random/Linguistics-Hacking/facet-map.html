Each tile is a facet; its color is how well we can measure it today. Hover a tile to inspect it, click to pin. This is the eBPF view of how humans, and machines, carry meaning.

How to read this map

Every facet is a place an idea gets encoded, located by three coordinates and marked by how well we can measure it. The grid is a stack: meaning on top, the raw wire at the bottom, one column per channel we transmit on.

Levels (the stack, rows)

channelthe wire: bytes, graphemes, tokens.
phonologicalsound system: the syllable, unit of the speech-rate model.
morphologicalword-internal structure: grammar packed per word.
lexicalword choice and vocabulary: richness, density.
syntacticsentence structure: how clauses nest.
discourseacross sentences: reference, cohesion, punctuation.
rhetoricalargument shape: framing, examples, redundancy.
semantic-pragmaticmeaning in context: what is conveyed, and inferred.

Modalities (columns) and the duality gate

The gate asks whether a channel is doubly articulated (meaningless units recombine into meaningful ones). Channels that pass carry language-grade information; the rest carry affect-grade information.

Amodal- · not a carrier; the bucket for facets that hold regardless of channel (e.g. proposition count).
Speech- · phonemes -> morphemes -> words -> clauses. the canonical doubly-articulated channel.
Writing- · graphemes -> words -> sentences. includes the human-to-machine encoding layer (tokens).
Sign language- · full grammatical language in the visual modality; handshape/location/movement are sublexical (Stokoe 1960; Brentari 2019).
Co-speech gesture- · imagistic and gradient, not segmented into recombinable units; complements speech rather than encoding independently (McNeill 2005).
Facial expression[|~] partial · affect-grade by default; doubly articulated and GRAMMATICAL inside sign languages (brow/head mark conditionals, questions). a signal that changes level by population.
Paralanguage (prosody, tone, volume)- · gradient affect/emphasis carrier; intonation has structure but is not segmental in the duality sense.
Music (melody, notation)[|~] partial · a sonic-symbolic channel distinct from speech and paralanguage; conveys affect and structure. has hierarchical/recursive structure debated as syntax-like (Lerdahl & Jackendoff 1983), but whether it is doubly articulated is contested. its notation is the iconographic/written rendering.
Iconography (emoji, glyphs, notation)[|~] partial · emoji are largely paralinguistic (not compositional/productive); logographic scripts and math notation do pass, but those sit under writing. mixed by instance (Evans 2017).
Touch (Protactile, Braille)[|~] partial · Braille is writing rendered to touch (passes); Protactile is an emerging doubly-articulated tactile language under DeafBlindness. touch-as-affect does not pass.
Non-human communication[|~] partial · animal signal systems (whale codas, birdsong, primate calls, the bee waggle dance). mostly NO duality (holistic signals), but contested partial cases exist - sperm whale codas show combinatorial structure (Sharma 2024) and birdsong has phonological syntax without semantics (Berwick 2011). the human exception is having BOTH duality and productivity (Hockett 1960). spans acoustic and non-acoustic (kinetic waggle dance) channels.
