POCSAG (Post Office Code Standardisation Advisory Group) is the paging protocol behind decades of pagers, and it's still what hospital and public-safety paging networks run today - not out of nostalgia, but because a one-way broadcast with strong forward error correction reaches through concrete stairwells a phone signal can't. Every codeword is exactly 32 bits, with a BCH code strong enough to correct errors without ever asking the transmitter to resend anything - there's no back-channel to resend on. A pager spends most of its life asleep. The preamble exists purely to let its receiver's clock lock on before anything meaningful arrives - functionally the same job Ethernet's preamble does, just far longer because a pager's local oscillator is far cheaper and less stable.

preamble sample (4 of 72 bytes - real preamble is 576 bits)
batch structure
Preamble (≥576 bits, alternating 1010...) -> Sync codeword 0x7CD215D8 -> Frame 0: address codeword, message codeword(s) -> Frame 1: address codeword, message codeword(s) -> ... -> Frame 7: address codeword, message codeword(s) -> next batch's sync codeword, or end of transmission

17 codewords per batch: 1 sync plus 8 frames of 2 codewords each. A pager only has to wake up and listen during its own assigned frame - the low 3 bits of its address pick which of the 8 frames that is, so a receiver sleeps through 7/8 of every batch.

sync and idle codewords (8 bytes)
why "idle," not silence. A slot with nothing to send still transmits the idle codeword (0x7A89C197) rather than going quiet - keeping every receiver's bit and frame synchronization locked for the whole batch, since losing sync would cost far more airtime to reacquire than just filling the gap.
exampleRIC 1000, frame 0function00 - numeric

A pager's real address (its "RIC," 21 bits) splits in two: the low 3 bits pick which of the 8 frames to listen in (not carried in the codeword at all - it's implicit in the frame position), and the remaining 18 bits ride here.

address codeword (32 bits)
computed, not asserted. The 10 BCH bits and the parity bit here are the real output of a BCH(31,21) encoder (generator polynomial x¹⁰+x⁵+x⁴+x⁶+x⁵+x³+1) run in Python against this exact address+function payload, then independently re-divided to confirm a zero remainder - not filled in by hand.
data20 bits, illustrativeeccsame BCH(31,21)

Follows an address codeword with the same frame's message content - a numeric page packs 4 bits per digit (not quite textbook BCD - real encoders reserve a few 4-bit codes for space and punctuation), an alphanumeric page packs 7-bit ASCII across as many codewords as needed.

message codeword (32 bits)
Message data shown here (0xCAFE0) is an illustrative bit pattern, not a decoded digit string - the point of this tab is the codeword shape, not a specific page's content.

POCSAG outlived the consumer pager entirely - it's now a protocol almost exclusively found in the infrastructure built to be more reliable than a cell network, not less.
